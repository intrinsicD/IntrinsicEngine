## Q1: Which application papers add something to BCPD (constraints, preprocessing, priors, hybrids), and what?

### Takeaway
Real algorithmic changes to BCPD cluster in four places. (1) Computer-assisted orthopaedic surgery: the Min, Zhang and Meng group (CUHK/HKUST) extended it to 6D positions plus normals, anisotropic noise and bidirectional variants. (2) Abdominal multi-organ CT: MO-BCPD. (3) Topology change: Break-and-Splice clusters with a DPGMM, then runs BCPD per cluster. (4) Hirose's own extensions: geodesic kernel, BCPD++ and the 2026 DET for function-valued data. Most other "applications" use BCPD/BCPD++/GBCPD as-is: for correspondence in shape models, FE-mesh morphing, US-CT refinement, robot cutting and microscopy. I found no paper that modifies BCPD itself for rope/cloth tracking, plants/agriculture, dental, lung or brain. Those domains use CPD-family alternatives or plain BCPD.

### Inferences
- Robotics DLO/cloth tracking has stayed with CPD/GLTP-derived trackers (CDCPD, TrackDLO) and does not use BCPD. The likely reason is that the VB updates and the full GP prior are heavier per frame than CPD/GLTP, and those trackers need explicit topology/occlusion priors. This is an inference: no source states it.
- Successful clinical uses (liver US-CT, kidney US-CT) mostly use BCPD's rigid/similarity mode or as a refinement after a global initializer. That is consistent with BCPD being a local method whose advantage is a wider rotation basin than CPD, not global optimality.

### Gaps
- No BCPD-specific modification found for: lung CT / DIR-Lab / PVT1010 (RobOT, NeurIPS 2021, uses CPD-family baselines; BCPD was not confirmed among them), dental/intraoral, brain-shift, plant phenotyping (Chebrolu/Magistri use skeleton-based registration), rope/cloth/DLO tracking. OpenAlex filters "rope", "cloth", "plant", "lung", "dental", "tooth" over BCPD citers returned no relevant hit (only "Dynamic Point Fields", ICCV 2023, for "cloth").
- Not retrievable here (paywalled or abstract missing): full method details of MO-BCPD, the film-cooling-hole BCPD method (AEI 2026), Visual Computer Student's-t BCPD and the 2026 Pattern Recognition VB method.

## Q2: What do comparative studies report on accuracy, robustness to noise/outliers/partiality, runtime and failure modes of BCPD?

### Takeaway
Three patterns hold across independent benchmarks. BCPD reliably beats CPD in rotation robustness and convergence. It is competitive and fast at moderate scale (below about 5×10^5 points). It is clearly beaten on large articulated deformations (FAUST/TOSCA), partial-to-partial scans (4DMatch/4DLoMatch) and outlier-biased partial overlap, by GBCPD, neural-field methods (NDP, NSFP, Nerfies), learned matchers (Lepard) and robust-OT/partial-matching methods (PWAN). Its accuracy on 4DMatch is about at CPD level.

### Cited Findings
- **The original BCPD paper vs. CPD, TPS-RPM, GMMReg:** for all datasets, BCPD had the widest range of initial rotation angles with correct registration. Under clustered outliers, BCPD and CPD were "moderately similar" in accuracy. FilterReg was not compared — [Hirose 2021, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339084651_A_Bayesian_Formulation_of_Coherent_Point_Drift)
- **NDP (Y. Li, T. Harada, NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2205.12796), Table 1:**

  | Method | 4DMatch EPE | AccS | AccR | Outlier | 4DLoMatch EPE | AccS | AccR | Outlier | Time (s) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | CPD | 0.274 | 1.57 | 7.30 | 74.52 | 0.463 | 0.07 | 0.48 | 84.49 | 4.52 |
  | BCPD | 0.291 | 5.13 | 12.35 | 73.74 | 0.492 | 0.20 | 0.86 | 86.88 | 5.81 |
  | NDP | 0.195 | 18.69 | 35.64 | 45.04 | 0.467 | 0.79 | 3.05 | 80.47 | 2.31 |
  | Lepard+SVD | 0.137 | 6.91 | 24.50 | 43.43 | 0.160 | 5.27 | 19.77 | 44.16 | 0.06 |

  BCPD has a higher accuracy rate than CPD but a slightly worse EPE, and is far behind learned/neural methods on partial-to-partial data — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12796)
- **UCA (M. Zhao, J. Jiang, L. Ma, S. Xin, G. Meng, D.-M. Yan, CVPR 2024 Highlight, DOI 10.1109/CVPR52733.2024.02003, arXiv 2406.18817):**
  - FAUST average RMSE: BCPD 0.0888 (3.04 s), GBCPD 0.0265 (1.93 s), Fast RNRR 0.0557, AMM NRR 0.0511, Sinkhorn 0.1170, NDP 0.0174, NSFP 0.0143, Nerfies 0.0129 (9.43 s), UCA 0.0092.
  - TOSCA (large deformations) average RMSE: BCPD 5.99, GBCPD 2.65, NDP 3.26, NSFP 2.07, Nerfies 2.32, UCA 1.52.
  - 2D human (subjects 1–4): BCPD 0.103–0.158 at 0.69 s vs. GMM 0.057–0.092.
  - Critique: with BCPD's random-sampling Nyström "it is unclear how effective the Nyström approximation performs", and the switch to KD-tree when σ² becomes small "may affect the optimization trajectory" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.18817)
- **PWAN (Z.-M. Wang, N. Xue, L. Lei, G.-S. Xia, ICLR 2022, arXiv 2203.02227; TPAMI 2025, DOI 10.1109/TPAMI.2025.3572795):**
  - Compared with CPD, GMM-REG, BCPD and TPS-RPM.
  - Scalability: "BCPD is the fastest method when q is small", and 2-GPU PWAN overtakes BCPD only above 5×10^5 points.
  - On outliers and partial overlap, CPD and BCPD "bias toward outliers".
  - On human scans, "CPD and BCPD can handle local deformations relatively well", but "have difficulties aligning point sets with large articulated deformations" near the limbs.
  - In partial-overlap tests "both CPD and BCPD fail ... they both wrongly match the left arm to the body".
  - BCPD parameters (β, λ, w) were set per dataset following Hirose's suggestions, e.g. (2.0, 2.0, 0.1), (3.0, 20.0, 0.1), (0.3, 2.0, 0.1) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02227)
- **GBCPD paper itself:** compares GBCPD, GBCPD++, CPD, BCPD, Fast RNRR, AMM NRR (3D) and PR-GLS, RPM-L2E (2D). It shows that CPD/BCPD unnaturally deform neighbouring parts (e.g., legs) and that GBCPD++ scales to several million points (author claim) — [OpenAlex abstract](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/TPAMI.2022.3214191)

### Inferences
- On full-body/articulated shapes, GBCPD cuts BCPD's error by roughly 3× (FAUST 0.0888 → 0.0265; TOSCA 5.99 → 2.65 in UCA's table). Most of BCPD's large-deformation weakness therefore comes from the Euclidean Gaussian kernel, not from the VB machinery.
- No study I found includes BCPD, FilterReg, GMMReg, TPS-RPM, FLOT, NDP and DefTransNet in a single head-to-head. The comparisons are pairwise or partial, and the parameter tuning protocols differ.

## Q3: Known limitations of BCPD reported by others, and proposed fixes

### Takeaway
Documented limitations:
- Euclidean-kernel motion coherence fuses neighbouring parts (legs, arms to body). Fix: geodesic kernel (GBCPD), clustering (Break-and-Splice), spectral bases.
- Sensitivity to outliers and partial overlap, with bias toward outliers. Fixes: partial OT / PWAN, overlap-guided mixtures, learned overlap.
- The asymmetric noise model (one set assumed noise-free). Fix: bidirectional/generalized variants.
- Isotropic noise and position-only features. Fix: normals + anisotropic covariances (GBCPD/AGBCPD).
- Local minima / need for initialization. Fix: global initializers (kidney US-CT).
- Hyperparameters (β, λ, w, γ) tuned per dataset.
- The Nyström-to-KD-tree acceleration switch, and limited control at more than 50k points.
- Redundant rigid + non-rigid parametrization (a criticism from SAM/SA-CPD).

I found no third-party source documenting "scale collapse" or symmetric-shape flips specifically for BCPD.

### Cited Findings
- **Neighbouring parts deform unnaturally because of proximity-based motion coherence:** fixed by geodesic motion coherence (GBCPD/GBCPD++) — [Hirose TPAMI 2022 abstract](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/TPAMI.2022.3214191). Independently confirmed by PWAN (limb errors; left arm matched to body) — [arXiv 2203.02227](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02227)
- **Large/articulated deformation:** BCPD's TOSCA RMSE is 5.99 vs. 1.52–2.65 for GBCPD and neural/clustering methods — [UCA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.18817). RMA-Net: CPD/BCPD "can not handle large-scale non-rigid deformation" — [S2 context](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2020.2971687/citations)
- **Topology change and large inter-frame motion:** handled by DPGMM clustering + per-cluster BCPD (Break and Splice, CGF 2023) — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1111/cgf.14788)
- **Outliers and partial overlap:** CPD and BCPD "bias toward outliers" and fail on partial sets (PWAN) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02227). SCALP (arXiv 2608.00187, 2026) says the soft smoothness penalties of CPD, BCPD and NICP "routinely collapse under outlier noise or missing regions", and replaces them with band-limited spectral bases (citation context) — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2020.2971687/citations)
- **Asymmetric noise assumption:** "one point set is perfect while the other contains noise and outliers". Fixes: bidirectional hybrid mixture models (BiAGCPD, BHMR, OBHMR, DeepBHMR) — [S2 contexts](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2020.2971687/citations)
- **Isotropic noise and no normals:** fixed by GBCPD (IROS 2021) and AGBCPD (TASE 2023) — [UCL PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10150628/1/Min_TASE_AGBCPD_final_submission.pdf)
- **Local minima / initialization:** "prone to local minima" (UniRiT 2024; GERA, ICRA 2025). Kidney US-CT puts global registration in front of BCPD — [S2 contexts](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2020.2971687/citations)
- **Acceleration side-effects:**
  - Random-sampling Nyström quality is unclear, and the KD-tree switch at small σ² perturbs the trajectory; UCA replaces it with a k-means-based Nyström — [UCA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.18817)
  - Survey: downsampling/interpolation "may degrade" accuracy (Artif. Intell. Rev. 2022) — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2020.2971687/citations)
  - Atlas registration: for more than 50k points "the acceleration possibilities are severely limited, along with the control options" — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2020.2971687/citations)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:**
  - PWAN tuned (β, λ, w) per dataset over ranges β 0.3–3.0 and λ 2–20 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02227)
  - ALPACA describes α (rigidity vs. fluidity) and β (directional correlation) trade-offs and sets them by exploration on a mouse dataset — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1111/2041-210X.13689)
  - Atlas paper: "manually tuned hyperparameters ... governing motion vector length and rigidity" — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2020.2971687/citations)
  - GBCPD "requires the tuning parameters of BCPD because it uses BCPD as a subroutine" — [S2 context of GBCPD](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2020.2971687/citations)
- **Redundant parametrization:** BCPD handles "rigid and non-rigid cases redundantly" (Structured Analytic Mappings, SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 2026). MO-BCPD, by contrast, keeps the redundancy because it improves rotation robustness — [S2 contexts](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2020.2971687/citations)
- **Speed vs. rigid CPD:** in CLEM-Reg "rigid CPD is faster and computationally less expensive than nonlinear BCPD" — [S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2020.2971687/citations). Conversely, BCPD is fastest at small and medium sizes in PWAN's scalability test — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02227)

### Inferences
- The fixes form a clear map. Kernel change (geodesic, ℓ1-Laplacian, spectral) addresses large/articulated deformation. Noise-model change (anisotropic, Student-t, bidirectional) addresses outliers and real sensors. Pre-segmentation (multi-organ, DPGMM clusters) addresses multi-object scenes and topology change. Front-end globalization (global initializers, learned correspondences) addresses local minima.
- For an engine implementation, the most transferable add-ons are: a geodesic/graph kernel option, normal-augmented likelihoods, an explicit partial-overlap/outlier model, and a deterministic, not random-sampling, low-rank approximation. All four are backed by at least one peer-reviewed comparison above.

### Gaps
- "Scale collapse" (the similarity scale s shrinking toward degenerate solutions) and failures on symmetric shapes: no third-party report found. If relevant, check the BCPD paper's own discussion of the γ/λ settings and the bcpd README.
- Formal sensitivity sweeps over β/λ/γ/w in an independent study: none found. The evidence is anecdotal tuning reports.
