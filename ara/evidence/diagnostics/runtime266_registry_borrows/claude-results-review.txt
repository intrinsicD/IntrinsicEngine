## Verdict: keep the change

**What the data supports.** The structural evidence is the solid part and it is independent of timing noise: identical 776 compiler invocations, no new files/objects, no layout or behavior change, full CPU suite (4663 pass + the one expected ASan skip), 408 focused, fresh Clang 20 whole-library build, 11/11 boundary guards. A global attachment of the sole `ECS.Scene.Registry` definition plus 10 borrow declarations, four explicit `cpp` imports, and one dead import removed is a cheap, reversible locality change costing +46 declaration/comment lines. On correctness and design grounds alone it holds.

**What the data does not support.** Do not claim 2.1% clean / 7.2% snapshot / 6.1% scene speedups. Every scenario's *before* pair is bimodal — one tight sample and one slow one (clean 334.6/346.9; snapshot 26.3/29.1; scene 36.4/40.0) — while all *after* pairs are tight. A single slow run contaminating every before median at n=2 is the signature of a machine-wide perturbation, not a per-scenario effect. The untouched producers confirm this: `Graphics.Renderer.cpp` (14387→14331), `Graphics.Renderer.cppm` (13012→12966), and `Geometry.Linalg.cpp` (9635→9664, *slower*) move ~0.3% or less, yet their before samples carry the same spread. Ranges for clean barely separate (334.6 vs 334.1, ~0.5 s), which is not a 2% claim.

The defensible statement is: **no regression, plus a small consistent directional improvement.** CPU seconds (1307.7→1279.9, ~2.1%) and max single-process RSS (2725.1→2718.9 MiB, non-overlapping and tiny-variance in every scenario) are the least noisy signals, and both move the right way. The RSS drop in particular is a real structural consequence — less duplicated registry AST per TU — and is not explainable by scheduler noise.

**Further interface splitting is not justified.** The isolated `ftime-trace` shows `WriteAST` 14.727 s of `ExecuteCompiler` 16.690 s, with `Frontend` only 1.924 s and `ReadAST` 28 ms across 8 module loads. Consuming imports are effectively free; the cost is serializing this one interface's own AST into the BMI. Note this trace ran outside the timing harness and is not a controlled comparison — it locates cost, it does not measure the change. But its implication is directional and clear: pruning or partitioning the *import* side attacks the 1.9 s, not the 14.7 s. Splitting `EditorWorkspaceSnapshots` into partitions would still write the same declarations; it only pays off if the split lets distinct consumers stop depending on the other half, and nothing here shows that. Don't build new abstractions on this evidence.

**Single best follow-up.** Run the `snapshot_interface` scenario alone, n≥5 interleaved ABBA, with `-ftime-trace` enabled *inside* the controlled harness, recording `WriteAST` ms and emitted BMI byte size per sample. That does two things at once: it gives enough samples to separate signal from the bimodal noise seen here, and it tests the one hypothesis that matters — whether build time on this path is a function of BMI serialization volume. If `WriteAST` tracks BMI size, the next lever is reducing what that interface exports, measurable directly and without restructuring anything first.
