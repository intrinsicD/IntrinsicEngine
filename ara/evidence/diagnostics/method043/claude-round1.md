**Round 1 critique (SDF field only)**

**1. Opposite-normal test is wrong as stated and will break the sphere control.** "Reject if hit normal · source normal ≥ 0" is not Shapira's back-face test. On a unit sphere, a ray at 60° from the inward axis exits the surface where the hit normal makes 60° with the source normal, dot = +0.5, so it is rejected. Every ray beyond 45° from the axis is thrown away on the sphere, and on any convex lobe the valid fraction collapses toward the inner ring. Use the ray direction instead: a hit is valid iff hit_normal · ray_dir > 0 (the ray exits the solid). Keep "nearest positive hit of the entire mesh, then reject, never skip" as written. On a clean closed manifold a rejected first hit can only come from the offset placing the origin outside or from a watertightness miss, so report the rejection count as a mesh/offset diagnostic and expect it to be near zero on the controls. Also exclude hits with t below a small multiple of the offset, not just the source triangle, since edge-adjacent triangles at 1e-9 can produce grazing self-hits.

**2. Ray layout without an axis ray biases upward and makes the box control not "exact".** For a locally slab-like region of thickness d, an oblique ray at polar angle θ returns d/cosθ, up to 2d at 60°. The median-band filter and inverse-angle weights reduce but do not remove this, and the residual factor depends on ring geometry. The bias is roughly multiplicative on slabs, so it barely affects ordering, which is what Round 2 needs, but the box-central-face prediction must be the value computed from the fixed ray set, not the box height. Simplest fix: include the axis ray with highest weight (31 rays), so box and cylinder controls have one exactly interpretable sample. Since the tangent frame comes from a triangle edge, the flipped 32x24 control will not reproduce per-face values; pre-register a distributional tolerance rather than per-face equality.

**3. What the field can and cannot tell you.** SDF is local inward diameter, not enclosed volume behind the face; two arms of equal radius are indistinguishable, which you already accept. Check the decorative-belt prediction arithmetically before running: a 12% radial belt on a sphere raises local diameter by about 24%, which is a genuine regional superlevel peak with relative prominence near your planned 0.25 cutoff. The belt control may therefore fail the "no high-prominence peak" prediction by construction, not by bug. State the expected belt prominence as roughly 2h/D up front. The 5% groove yields about a 10% depression and should stay below threshold. For the bent neck, expect an asymmetric field across the bend (outer wall sees longer oblique rays), so the thickness saddle shifts toward the inner bend; do not read that as failure.

**4. Smallest correct watershed to freeze before Round 2.** Work on the face-adjacency graph with face values f and face areas. Sort faces by (f descending, face index) for determinism. Union-find in that order. When a face joins two components, the one with the lower peak is the candidate to die, with persistence = (peak − f_current)/peak and basin area = its accumulated face area at that moment. It survives only if persistence ≥ 0.25 and area ≥ 0.02 of total area; otherwise it is absorbed. Plateaus: adjacent faces with equal f must be unioned before any death is recorded, or ties will spawn spurious peaks; process each distinct value as a batch. Invalid faces (fewer than 6 rays) must have a frozen rule, either −∞ (become valley) or neighbor-area-weighted fill, decided now. Use face area, not face count, throughout.

**5. Record for reproducibility.** Emit the fixed ray direction table, per-face valid count, rejection reason counts, and the offset scale in the output alongside the hash.
